## 4.2 System Descriptions

**S1. Too Good To Go.** Shops, cafés and bakeries list “surprise bags” of unsold food at about one-third of the original price; users reserve and collect them within a time window. The company reports about 130 million users and 180,000 partners in 20 countries. It does not operate in Kazakhstan and offers no collective purchasing or social status support.

**S2. OLIO.** Individuals and volunteers give away surplus food free of charge to nearby users via map-based listings. Research shows most users act only as donors or only as recipients (Harvey et al., 2020). It has no bulk purchasing or payments and is not available in Kazakhstan.

**S3. Community group buying in China.** A resident leader collects pre-orders in a WeChat group; the platform delivers the combined order the next day to one point, and neighbours collect their items. The model reduces last-mile costs and relies on trust in the leader (Wu et al., 2022). It depends on WeChat and Chinese logistics and offers no Kazakh language, food sharing or social module.

**S4. Arzan.kz.** Compares prices and promotions across Kazakhstani retail chains. It helps find discounts, but purchases remain individual and at retail prices.

**S5. Red Crescent Food Bank and community fridges.** The food bank, launched with METRO Cash & Carry, receives products from businesses and distributes them to vulnerable families; community fridges with free food have appeared in Almaty. It is charitable, not digital, and limited in reach; tax rules discourage business donations.

## 4.5 Conclusions

1. No existing system covers all criteria. Too Good To Go and OLIO address surplus food only, Chinese platforms address collective purchasing only, Arzan.kz addresses price search only, and the food bank addresses charitable distribution only.
2. There is no digital collective grocery purchasing platform in Kazakhstan; joint purchases are organised manually in messaging groups, with prepayment risk and disputes.
3. Social status support exists only in the non-digital food bank.
4. Adopted features: the organiser + combined order + pick-up point model (S3); time-limited discounted listings with a collection window (S1); a map of nearby offers (S2); priority for vulnerable families (S5).
5. The proposed system's unique value is the combination of district pools with automatic share calculation and Kaspi QR payment, B2C food sharing and social discounts in one bilingual application.
